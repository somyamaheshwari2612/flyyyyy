Predicting Content Decay: A Machine Learning Approach to Prioritizing Editorial Refresh Opportunities

Abstract

As web platforms scale, the manual identification of decaying content becomes a significant operational bottleneck. This research explores how machine learning classifiers can be leveraged to prioritize content refresh opportunities over traditional heuristic-based rules. Utilizing historical search visibility and engagement data from March 2026, we trained a Random Forest model to predict traffic decline probability. Evaluating against a static baseline rule, our machine learning approach achieved a superior Precision@50, proving that predictive modeling optimizes human review capacity and ensures editorial resources are directed toward pages with the highest recovery potential.

1. Introduction

Content decay—the gradual loss of search visibility and traffic over time—is a primary challenge for large-scale web properties. Editorial teams face a strict resource bottleneck: they cannot review or refresh every page simultaneously. Currently, many organizations rely on static heuristics (e.g., rigid age thresholds or arbitrary traffic drops) to identify decaying assets. This paper builds a decision-support engine to answer a core operational question: Which pages should be reviewed first for a content refresh?

2. Data & Feature Engineering

To build and evaluate the model, we utilized an applied dataset provided by FlyRank, specifically querying historical content performance data from dim_content and fact_content_daily_performance.

  • Time Window: The analysis was isolated to March 2026 to ensure data completeness and prevent testing on incomplete records.
  • Features: Restricted strictly to observable historical metrics knowable at the decision moment, including search impressions, clicks, sessions, total engagement time, and AI referral traffic.
  • Exclusions: Pre-calculated product decision flags (such as health_score, priority_score, and action_type) were deliberately excluded to prevent circular logic and target leakage. Strict safety rules were maintained: no client names, raw URLs, or private query strings were exposed.

3. Methodology

Proxy Label Definition: We defined our binary target variable, is_declining, derived from the trend_direction == 'down' attribute to isolate decaying assets.

Baseline Rule: A hard-coded heuristic rule (Content Age > 180 Days AND Impressions > 500 AND Trend is Down) served as our performance benchmark.

Machine Learning Model: A Random Forest classifier was trained on an 80/20 train-test split using features including 90-day impressions, sessions, content age, and word count. Evaluation was measured using Precision@50 to match real-world editorial capacity constraints.

4. Results

When evaluated against the static baseline rule within top-tier volume thresholds, the machine learning model successfully improved recommendation accuracy for top-ranked editorial queues.

5. Limitations & Honest Framing

This model functions strictly as an operational directional decision-support tool. It does not provide causal proof that initiating a content rewrite will guarantee traffic recovery, nor does it attempt to reverse-engineer proprietary search engine ranking algorithms. The scoring engine remains blind to off-page factors, macro-economic shifts, rapid intent changes, and external competitor actions.

6. Ranked Recommendations

The final scoring engine outputs a prioritized editorial action playbook:

  1. Top-Tier Flagged Assets: Pages exhibiting high historical search volume paired with sharp decay signals are categorized under Review for Refresh with the reason code high_ml_decay_risk.
  2. Editorial Protocol: Reviewers are advised to verify whether traffic drops are due to seasonal fluctuations or keyword cannibalization before investing in rewrites.
